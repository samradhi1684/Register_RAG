RegisterRAG vs the Baselines

Where RegisterRAG beats each published baseline and where it doesn't, which question types decide it, and a close look at DSIRE, the one dataset where plain chunk retrieval still wins.

Summary

RegisterRAG (qwen2.5:7b) was compared with each baseline's qwen2.5:7b run on the questions both answered: 429 on 2Wiki, 454 on HotpotQA, 450 on MuSiQue, 398 on DSIRE. Token F1 is the headline metric everywhere on this page: it gives partial credit for answers that differ from the gold only in wording, and works for both short and long-form answers. EM is shown beside it as a stricter, secondary check. A difference counts only when its 95% paired bootstrap interval excludes zero. On F1, RegisterRAG is reliably better in 35 of 60 comparisons, reliably worse in 4, and within noise in the rest.

  • Against the strongest baseline: level or behindCompared with each dataset's single best configuration, RegisterRAG is level on 2Wiki (+0.027 [-0.024, +0.075]) and MuSiQue (-0.047 [-0.092, +0.001]) with about half the LLM calls, and behind on HotpotQA and DSIRE. Where it loses, it is the reading of evidence, not the retrieval (see vs strongest baseline).
  • 2Wiki: a large, clear winRegisterRAG scores 0.642 F1 (0.564 EM) and beats 15 of 16 baselines, by +0.219 over B1. Only LiveLedger stays within noise (+0.027 [-0.024, +0.075]).
  • MuSiQue: ahead of everything except the search loop0.346 F1 (0.242 EM), +0.114 F1 over B1. Both LiveLedger variants are level with it (-0.047 [-0.092, +0.001] against the no-ledger loop). On 4-hop questions RegisterRAG leads B1 by +0.131 [+0.060, +0.198] F1 and is level with the no-ledger loop (0.277 vs 0.278).
  • HotpotQA: no advantageRetrieval is already saturated. RegisterRAG (0.619) is level with B1 (-0.028 [-0.074, +0.020]) and reliably behind both LiveLedger variants (-0.053 [-0.094, -0.008]).
  • DSIRE: B1 is reliably betterRegisterRAG's F1 (0.491) is -0.053 against B1. It loses most on yes/no, where an unsupported requirement becomes "No": it misses 33% of gold-"yes" questions against 22% for B1.
  • The register does the workWhen all of RegisterRAG's requirements are supported by evidence, it scores higher on every dataset: 0.410 vs 0.240 F1 on MuSiQue, 0.655 vs 0.535 on 2Wiki. Support coverage is a usable confidence signal.
  • What to borrow from the baselinesLiveLedger's adaptive re-query is the only baseline feature that matches or beats RegisterRAG on multi-hop data. B1's whole-paragraph context is what wins DSIRE yes/no.

RegisterRAG vs the strongest baseline on each dataset

The sections above compare RegisterRAG with every baseline. This one asks a harder question: how does it do against the single strongest baseline on each dataset? "Strongest" means the 7B configuration with the highest F1 on the shared questions. It is picked after seeing the results, which favours the baseline, so this is a conservative test. All differences are RegisterRAG minus the strongest baseline in token F1, paired, with 95% bootstrap intervals. The agreement matrices and McNemar tests treat F1 ≥ 0.5 as correct.

Key findings

  • 1 · Level on 2Wiki and MuSiQue, behind on HotpotQA and DSIRERegisterRAG minus the strongest: 2Wiki +0.027 [-0.024, +0.075], MuSiQue -0.047 [-0.092, +0.001], HotpotQA -0.068 [-0.109, -0.025], DSIRE -0.053 [-0.093, -0.010]. McNemar agrees: p 0.003 on HotpotQA and 0.007 on DSIRE; not significant on the other two.
  • 2 · The bar is an iterative search loop, at about twice the callsA LiveLedger variant is the strongest baseline on 2Wiki, HotpotQA and MuSiQue; on DSIRE it is one-shot B1. RegisterRAG matches LiveLedger on 2Wiki with 3.4 LLM calls per question against 5.6, and on MuSiQue with 2.3 against 4.9.
  • 3 · Retrieval is not the gap; reading isRegisterRAG finds every gold document more often than the strongest baseline on HotpotQA (93% vs 87%) and MuSiQue (48% vs 37%). On the questions where both retrieved every gold document it still trails: HotpotQA -0.067 [-0.111, -0.023], DSIRE -0.054 [-0.098, -0.011], MuSiQue -0.092 [-0.190, +0.004].
  • 4 · Decomposition fidelity decides the outcomeWhen RegisterRAG writes as many requirements as the question has gold hops, it beats LiveLedger on 2Wiki (+0.093 [+0.040, +0.147]). Too few requirements costs -0.234 on 2Wiki; too many costs -0.149 on HotpotQA and -0.240 on MuSiQue.
  • 5 · The "No" bias is not a DSIRE quirkGold "yes" answered "No": 2Wiki 16 of 32 (strongest 11), HotpotQA 13 of 32 (9), DSIRE 36 of 113 (22). It is a property of how RegisterRAG answers, not of policy text.
  • 6 · The errors are complementary, and the register says when to deferA per-question oracle of the two systems reaches 0.788 F1 on 2Wiki and 0.778 on HotpotQA. Sending only RegisterRAG's partly supported questions to the strongest baseline gives 0.661 on 2Wiki (+0.047 [-0.001, +0.093] over the strongest alone) and halves the HotpotQA gap.

Per-question outcomes and paired agreement

Most questions are decided the same way by both systems: 60% on 2Wiki, 66% on HotpotQA, 56% on MuSiQue, 44% on DSIRE have identical F1, usually both right or both wrong. The comparison rests on the remaining questions.

Among those, RegisterRAG wins more than it loses only on 2Wiki (94 vs 79). On HotpotQA it loses 96 and wins 58. On MuSiQue and DSIRE wins and losses are close in number, but the losses are larger.

The agreement matrices below show the same picture with binary correctness. Large off-diagonal cells on every dataset mean the two systems fail on different questions, which is what makes routing worthwhile (finding 6).

By question type

Only 1 of 17 type-level differences is reliable: bridge on HotpotQA (-0.095 [-0.158, -0.034]), in the baseline's favour. Two more come close: MuSiQue 3hop1 (-0.098 [-0.198, +0.005]) and DSIRE Yes-No (-0.107 [-0.227, +0.013]). Per type, RegisterRAG and the strongest baseline are otherwise statistically tied; the per-type samples (21–231 questions) are too small to separate differences under about 0.1.

RegisterRAG leans ahead on 2Wiki comparison and inference questions (comparison +0.061, inference +0.057), where a fixed list of attributes or relations is the right plan. It leans behind on questions whose structure is open-ended: bridge questions and 3-hop chains, where the iterative loop can follow whatever the first hop returns.

Retrieval is not the gap: reading is

If RegisterRAG lost because it found less evidence, the gap would close when both systems have all the gold documents. It doesn't. On HotpotQA it stays at -0.067, on DSIRE at -0.054, and on MuSiQue it grows to -0.092 (0.528 vs 0.620, n = 124).

So with the same evidence available, the strongest baseline turns it into the right answer more often. RegisterRAG's own failure labels point the same way: on the MuSiQue questions it loses, 64 of 108 are evidence_selection (the evidence was in the pool but not attached to the requirement) and only 24 are retrieval failures.

2Wiki is the exception. There, with all gold documents present, RegisterRAG leans ahead (0.701 vs 0.654; +0.047 [-0.010, +0.105]), so on comparison-structured questions the register does help reading.

Decomposition fidelity

A correctly sized register is RegisterRAG's real advantage. On 2Wiki, 319 of 429 questions get one requirement per hop, and on those RegisterRAG beats LiveLedger by +0.093 [+0.040, +0.147] (0.682 vs 0.589).

Too few requirements leaves a hop unplanned. On 2Wiki this happens on 62 questions, 50 of them bridge_comparison (4-hop), and costs -0.234 [-0.387, -0.081]. On MuSiQue it is common (153 questions) but costs little (-0.042), because the loop also struggles there.

Too many requirements is rarer but worse: HotpotQA -0.149 [-0.259, -0.037] on 76 questions, MuSiQue -0.240 [-0.401, -0.077] on 34. Each extra requirement is one more chance for a support check to fail or for the composer to pick up the wrong span.

The planner's hop estimate is available before answering, so a cheap check (does the plan have more steps than the question has entities?) could catch over-decomposition at inference time.

Answer-type confusion matrices

These extend the DSIRE yes/no matrices to every dataset and add the "other" class: a question whose gold answer is an entity but is answered "yes" or "no" (or the reverse) is a form error that no amount of retrieval fixes. MuSiQue has no yes/no gold answers, so only its "other" row appears.

  • Gold "yes" → "No" is RegisterRAG's dominant yes/no error on all three datasets that have yes/no questions, and it makes it more often than the strongest baseline each time (2Wiki 16 vs 11, HotpotQA 13 vs 9, DSIRE 36 vs 22). On 2Wiki this is half of all gold-"yes" comparison questions. Both systems almost never say "yes" to a gold "no".
  • Answering an entity question with yes/no (the "Gold other" row) is a HotpotQA-specific RegisterRAG problem: 20 times against 11 for the strongest baseline. Questions such as "Which of these men…?" get "No". On 2Wiki and MuSiQue it is the iterative loop that does this more (13 and 29 times, against 2 and 11 for RegisterRAG). On MuSiQue LiveLedger answers "no" to questions that never have a yes/no answer.
  • A simple answer-type guard (yes/no only if the question starts with an auxiliary verb) should remove most of the "Gold other" errors for both systems.

Register support as a diagnostic, and a router

On 2Wiki the support signal separates RegisterRAG's wins from its losses. On fully supported questions it beats LiveLedger (+0.053 [+0.003, +0.108]). On the 48 partly supported ones it loses badly (-0.174 [-0.340, -0.014]). HotpotQA and MuSiQue show the same direction, with the partly supported gap 2–3× larger.

On DSIRE the signal carries no information (the gap is -0.051 supported, -0.055 unsupported). That fits the deep dive: there the support checker fails on evidence that is present, so "unsupported" doesn't mean "the evidence is missing".

The signal is available at inference time, so the router below is deployable. It is one fixed rule, not tuned on these results: keep RegisterRAG's answer when every requirement is supported, otherwise use the fallback's.

The router beats both of its parts on 2Wiki and closes most of the gap on MuSiQue (-0.019, within noise). Falling back to cheap B1 instead keeps the 2Wiki result level with LiveLedger (+0.017 [-0.033, +0.068]) at about 3.6 calls per question instead of 5.6, but loses on MuSiQue, where B1 is weak. The oracle column shows the headroom: it is 0.09–0.15 F1 above the better of the two systems on every dataset.

Accuracy per LLM call

On 2Wiki and MuSiQue RegisterRAG sits on the accuracy/cost frontier: no baseline with fewer calls comes close to it. The only systems level with it are LiveLedger variants using 1.6× (2Wiki) and 2.2× (MuSiQue) as many calls. On HotpotQA it is off the frontier: the no-ledger loop is more accurate at the same number of calls, and one-shot B1 (0.647) is more accurate with one call. On DSIRE B1 is also more accurate with one call, so RegisterRAG's extra calls buy nothing there.

Hop depth

On MuSiQue all three systems lose accuracy with depth, but differently. B1 collapses at 4 hops; RegisterRAG and the loop do not. RegisterRAG's deficit against the loop sits at 3 hops (-0.094 [-0.179, -0.012]); at 4 hops the two are level (-0.000 [-0.076, +0.076]). On 2Wiki the 4-hop questions are comparisons of two 2-hop chains, which are no harder than 2-hop questions for either multi-call system.

Key insights, dataset by dataset

2WikiMultiHopQA · vs B9 LiveLedger

  • Statistically level overall (+0.027 [-0.024, +0.075]), at 3.4 vs 5.6 LLM calls. This is the only dataset where RegisterRAG is the most accurate system in the study.
  • Its edge comes from questions with a correctly sized register (74% of questions, +0.093 [+0.040, +0.147]) and from fully supported registers (+0.053 [+0.003, +0.108]).
  • Its weakest subsets are 4-hop bridge-comparison questions planned with too few requirements (-0.234), partly supported registers (-0.174), and "No" answers to gold "yes" (16 of 32).
  • Routing partly supported questions to LiveLedger gives 0.661 F1, the best 2Wiki result available from these runs.

HotpotQA · vs B9 LiveLedger, no ledger

  • Reliably behind (-0.068 [-0.109, -0.025]; McNemar p 0.003) at the same cost (3.2 vs 3.3 calls). The loss is on bridge questions; comparison is level.
  • It is not a retrieval loss: RegisterRAG has all gold documents more often (93% vs 87%) and still trails when both do.
  • Three causes, each measured: over-decomposition (76 questions, -0.149); yes/no answers to entity questions (20 vs 11); and longer, less precise answers (2.8 vs 2.0 words, token precision 0.63 vs 0.72).
  • Even on fully supported questions it trails (-0.049 [-0.097, -0.002]), so better support checks alone won't close this gap; the answer composer needs work.

MuSiQue · vs B9 LiveLedger, no ledger

  • Level overall (-0.047 [-0.092, +0.001]) at less than half the calls (2.3 vs 4.9).
  • The deficit is on 3-hop questions (-0.094 [-0.179, -0.012]); 2- and 4-hop are level.
  • RegisterRAG retrieves better (all gold documents on 48% vs 37% of questions) but uses the evidence worse: 0.528 vs 0.620 F1 when both have it. Most of its losses are evidence_selection failures (64 of 108).
  • The rare over-decomposed questions are the worst case in the study (-0.240 [-0.401, -0.077]).

DSIRE · vs B1 Chunk RAG

  • Reliably behind (-0.053 [-0.093, -0.010]) with three times the calls. See the DSIRE deep dive.
  • Retrieval is identical (doc recall@10 0.98 vs 0.97), so the whole gap is answer composition: yes/no polarity and long answers (13.5 vs 5.0 words, precision 0.57 vs 0.72, recall 0.51 vs 0.51).
  • This is the only dataset where the support signal is uninformative; it is the support checker, not the evidence, that fails here.

Threats to validity for this section. The strongest baseline is chosen on the same questions it is evaluated on, which inflates it slightly (winner's curse). This works against RegisterRAG, so "level" results are, if anything, conservative. The F1 ≥ 0.5 threshold used for agreement and McNemar is a convention; the F1 bootstrap intervals don't depend on it. Why RegisterRAG wins where it wins

The register separates confident answers from unconfident ones. On every dataset, fully supported questions score higher. The gap is largest on MuSiQue (0.410 vs 0.240), where missing a hop is the main failure. The baselines have nothing like this: no record of which part of the question their evidence covers.

Coverage differs a lot by dataset: 89% fully supported on 2Wiki, 75% fully supported on HotpotQA, 62% fully supported on MuSiQue, 53% fully supported on DSIRE. DSIRE's low coverage is surprising, since its retrieval is near perfect. RegisterRAG's requirement check fails to confirm evidence that is in the policy page, and that is where its DSIRE losses come from (see the DSIRE deep dive).

2. Yes/no: unsupported requirements become "No"

77% of these gold answers are "yes". RegisterRAG gets 67% of them right against 78% for B1, while its accuracy on gold-"no" is high (94%). Its errors run one way: "No" when the answer is "yes".

The register explains it. Of the 38 gold-"yes" questions RegisterRAG got wrong, 68% had at least one unsupported requirement, against 36% of the ones it got right. B1 answered 68% of those same questions correctly, so the evidence was retrievable. RegisterRAG treated an unconfirmed condition as a negative answer. For eligibility questions ("Can schools receive up to $1,550…?"), missing confirmation is not evidence of "no".

RegisterRAG's errors run one way. 38 of its 40 errors are gold "yes" answered "No"; it says "yes" to a gold "no" only 2 times. B1 makes the same mistake less often (23 times).

RegisterRAG's "No" is unreliable. It says "No" 70 times and is right 32 times (46%). B1 says "No" 56 times and is right 33 (59%). Both are almost always right when they say "yes" (RegisterRAG 78 of 80, B1 91 of 92).

5. Dataset caveats that affect every DSIRE number

  • Label noise of about 6%. That share of gold evidence spans are page headings or under six words (three read "Duplicate Entry"). Differences under about 0.03 F1 are not meaningful.
  • EM doesn't work for long-form types. No system reaches 0.08 EM on Descriptive or Legal-Obligation. Use F1 or the judge.
  • Type labels overlap. About half of "Yes-No" questions also carry conditions, so Yes-No vs Yes-No-cond is not a clean split.

Bottom line for DSIRE. RegisterRAG's machinery targets missing evidence, a problem DSIRE doesn't have. It loses to B1 for two fixable reasons: it reads an unconfirmed eligibility condition as "No", and its multi-requirement answers add words outside the gold span. Two changes are likely to close most of the gap:

  • For yes/no questions with an unsupported requirement, answer from the full retrieved paragraph instead of defaulting to "No".
  • For long-form types, answer from the single best-supported span.

Then compare every system with the same LLM judge.

Reliability of these comparisons

  • Partial overlap. RegisterRAG's 500-question sets are type-balanced samples. 429 of its 2Wiki questions, 454 of its HotpotQA questions, 450 of its MuSiQue questions, 398 of its DSIRE questions are also in the baseline runs. All comparisons use only those; the other 269 RegisterRAG questions are left out.
  • Why F1 is the headline metric. EM gives zero credit to "Antoine" for "Anthony, Duke of Brabant" and to every long policy answer that is not word-for-word identical. On 2Wiki, HotpotQA and MuSiQue the EM and F1 verdicts are identical for every comparison. On DSIRE they disagree in 7 of 16 comparisons, because EM cannot score the long-form answer types at all. So F1 is the reference and EM a secondary check.
  • Same scorer for both sides. RegisterRAG's answers were re-scored with the harness's EM/F1 against the baselines' gold answers, aliases included. MuSiQue has answer aliases, so this moves RegisterRAG's MuSiQue EM from 0.329 (CSV) to 0.346. The other datasets are unchanged.
  • Generator. The CSVs don't record the model; qwen2.5:7b is taken from the project owner. The retrieval corpus and machine may differ from the baseline runs (pooled corpus, Windows paths), which can shift retrieval difficulty slightly.
  • Noise floor. Each system is a single run. Repeated baseline runs (SURE-RAG with the gate off is effectively B1) differ by about ±0.01, and a typical paired interval here is ±0.04–0.05.
  • Interpretations. "An unsupported requirement becomes No" and the bridge-question phrasing loss on HotpotQA are readings of the per-question records. They fit the counts but haven't been tested by an intervention.

What to improve in RegisterRAG

These are the changes the analysis points to, in rough order of expected payoff. Each comes with the evidence from this report that motivates it.

  1. Stop reading "unsupported" as "No" on yes/no questions. On DSIRE, RegisterRAG misses 33% of questions whose answer is "yes" (B1 misses 22%). 68% of those misses had a requirement the evidence didn't confirm, and B1 got 68% of the same questions right, so the evidence was retrievable. When a requirement stays unsupported, answer from the full retrieved paragraph instead of defaulting to "No".
  2. Re-query when a requirement stays unsupported. LiveLedger's open-ended search loop is the only baseline that matches or beats RegisterRAG on multi-hop data:
    • it beats RegisterRAG on HotpotQA (-0.053 and -0.068 F1, RegisterRAG minus LiveLedger);
    • it ties it on MuSiQue;
    • without its ledger it matches RegisterRAG on MuSiQue 4-hop questions (0.278 vs 0.277 F1) with no requirement list at all.
    Re-querying for a requirement that stays unsupported would let RegisterRAG combine both strengths. The HotpotQA result suggests the loop finds evidence a fixed requirement list misses.
  3. Answer long-form questions from the best-supported span. On DSIRE's descriptive questions, RegisterRAG's answers are 2.4× longer than B1's. Recall goes up (0.33 vs 0.26) but precision drops more (0.44 vs 0.65), because the extra words come from combining several requirement answers rather than the gold sentence.
  4. Don't over-decompose questions whose evidence is already easy to find. On HotpotQA and DSIRE, retrieval is already near perfect, so splitting the question adds nothing and can lose phrasing: HotpotQA bridge questions are -0.055 F1 vs B1. Consider a one-shot path, or a single requirement, when the first retrieval already covers the question.
  5. Improve how requirements are confirmed as supported. Support coverage is a strong confidence signal: fully supported questions score higher on every dataset (MuSiQue 0.41 vs 0.24 F1). But only 53% of DSIRE questions are fully supported, even though doc recall@10 there is 0.98. The requirement checker is failing to confirm evidence that is in the page.
  6. Use support coverage for selective answering or fallback. Partly supported answers are reliably worse, so hand those questions to a fallback (a B1-style full-context answer or another retrieval round), or flag them. Sending them to the strongest baseline gives 0.661 F1 on 2Wiki (+0.047 [-0.001, +0.093] over LiveLedger alone) and closes the MuSiQue gap to -0.019 (Table B). Confirm this on a held-out split before reporting it as a system result.
  7. Size the register to the question. One requirement per gold hop is where RegisterRAG beats the strongest baseline (+0.093 [+0.040, +0.147] on 2Wiki). Over-decomposition costs -0.149 on HotpotQA and -0.240 on MuSiQue; under-decomposition of 4-hop 2Wiki questions costs -0.234 (Figure D). Check the plan length against the number of entities and relations in the question before retrieving.
  8. Add an answer-type guard. RegisterRAG answers yes/no to 20 HotpotQA entity questions (strongest baseline 11). Allow a yes/no answer only when the question is phrased as a yes/no question, and otherwise compose an entity answer from the best-supported requirement.
  9. Fix answer composition, not just retrieval. With every gold document retrieved by both systems, RegisterRAG still trails the strongest baseline on HotpotQA (-0.067 [-0.111, -0.023]) and DSIRE (-0.054 [-0.098, -0.011]) (Figure C), and its token precision is lower on HotpotQA, MuSiQue and DSIRE. The final composer should answer from the requirement that resolves the question, not combine every requirement's answer.
  10. Measure fairly before tuning further.
    • Score all systems with the same LLM judge; RegisterRAG's DSIRE judge score is 0.593 against 0.491 F1.
    • Rerun on the baselines' exact 500 questions (and at 3B), on the same machine, so every question is covered and latency is comparable.
